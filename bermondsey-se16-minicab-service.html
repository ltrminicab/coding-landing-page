Discover Bermondsey SE16, South London's Riverside Quarter

Bermondsey SE16 occupies a significant stretch of the south bank of the Thames in the London Borough of Southwark, a postcode that has undergone one of the most dramatic transformations of any inner-London district over the past three decades. The northern part of the postcode — centred on Bermondsey Street, one of London's oldest thoroughfares — has developed into one of south London's most celebrated cultural quarters, attracting galleries, independent restaurants, artisan producers, and creative businesses to a street whose origins predate the Norman Conquest. The White Cube Gallery on Bermondsey Street is among the most significant contemporary art spaces in the capital, drawing visitors from across London and internationally, while the Bermondsey Antiques Market on Tower Bridge Road has operated since 1949 and remains one of the largest open-air antiques markets in Europe.

The Rotherhithe neighbourhood in the southern part of SE16 has its own distinct identity rooted in the postcode's maritime heritage — this stretch of the Thames was one of the busiest docking and shipbuilding areas in Britain from the medieval period through to the late 20th century. The Brunel Museum at Rotherhithe records the construction of the first tunnel beneath the Thames, completed by Marc and Isambard Kingdom Brunel between 1825 and 1843 and now carrying the London Overground. Canada Water, developed on the former Surrey Commercial Docks, is currently one of the largest regeneration projects in Europe, with a new town centre, library, leisure facilities, and thousands of new homes planned across the former docklands. The postcode is served by Bermondsey and Canada Water stations on the Jubilee line, along with Rotherhithe, Surrey Quays, and South Bermondsey stations on the Overground network.
