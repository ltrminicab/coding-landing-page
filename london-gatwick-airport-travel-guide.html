The Complete Gatwick Airport Travel Guide for International Travellers — Step by Step

London Gatwick Airport (LGW) is the United Kingdom's second largest international airport and one of the busiest single-runway airports in the world. Situated approximately 28 miles south of Central London in West Sussex, Gatwick serves over 220 destinations across 90 countries, handling more than 260,000 aircraft movements each year. On a typical day, roughly 376 flights arrive at Gatwick — that is one landing approximately every four minutes. Whether you are heading to Barcelona for a city break, boarding a long-haul flight to New York, or catching a connection to Dubai, Gatwick is a major gateway that millions of passengers pass through every year.

For travellers departing from or arriving into London, the journey to and from Gatwick is one of the most important parts of the trip to plan correctly. A missed transfer, a confusing terminal change, or an unexpected traffic delay can turn an exciting holiday into an unnecessary ordeal. This step-by-step guide has been written by the team at LTR Minicab — a London-based, fully licensed private-hire cab company — to walk every type of traveller through the complete Gatwick experience from the moment you book your cab to the moment you reach your destination.

Step 1 — Understand Gatwick's Two Terminals Before You Travel

Gatwick Airport is split into two separate terminals: the North Terminal and the South Terminal. These are not adjacent buildings — they are connected by a dedicated shuttle train that runs 24 hours a day and takes approximately two to three minutes between terminals. However, arriving at the wrong terminal with heavy luggage and a tight departure window is a stressful and avoidable mistake.

Before you book your cab, check your airline and booking confirmation to identify which terminal your flight departs from or arrives into. As a general guide:

  • South Terminal — easyJet, British Airways (some routes), TUI, Vueling, and most charter airlines predominantly operate from here.
  • North Terminal — British Airways (most routes), Norwegian, Wizz Air, Virgin Atlantic, and Emirates are typically based here.
  • Always confirm with your airline directly as terminal allocations do change seasonally.

When you book with LTR Minicab, our booking system asks for your terminal so your driver drops you at exactly the right set-down point, saving time and eliminating last-minute panic. For arrivals, your driver meets you at the correct terminal arrivals hall with a name board so you are never left searching.

Step 2 — Book Your Gatwick Airport Transfer in Advance

The single biggest mistake Gatwick travellers make is leaving the ground transfer to the last minute. Taxis hailed outside the terminal at peak times are consistently more expensive and unreliable compared to a pre-booked private hire cab with a fixed fare. LTR Minicab recommends booking your Gatwick transfer at least 24 to 48 hours before your journey, and for early morning departures (before 6am), booking several days in advance to guarantee vehicle availability.

When booking, have the following information ready:

  • Your full pickup address (home, hotel, or business)
  • Your flight number and departure or arrival time
  • Your terminal (North or South)
  • The number of passengers travelling
  • The total number and size of luggage bags
  • Any special requirements such as child seats, extra legroom, or wheelchair-accessible vehicles

At LTR Minicab, all Gatwick airport transfer prices are fixed and confirmed at the time of booking. There are no surge charges, no hidden fees, and no unexpected extras when you arrive at your destination. You can get an instant quote online 24 hours a day.

Step 3 — Plan Your Departure Timing Carefully

Gatwick Airport recommends arriving at least two hours before a short-haul departure and three hours before a long-haul departure. For busy travel periods such as school holidays, bank holiday weekends, and peak summer season (June to August), add an extra 30 to 45 minutes to allow for longer check-in and security queues.

When planning your cab pickup time, factor in the road journey time from your location to Gatwick. Road travel to Gatwick can be affected by traffic on the M23, M25, A23, and A264 corridors — particularly during morning rush hour (7am to 9:30am) and evening peak (4:30pm to 7pm). LTR Minicab drivers are experienced in monitoring live traffic conditions and will adjust departure times accordingly, but as a passenger you should always build in a time buffer.

Step 5 — Navigating Gatwick Airport on Arrival

Once you arrive at Gatwick, the layout is straightforward for first-time visitors provided you understand the basic structure. Both terminals are self-contained with their own check-in halls, departure lounges, security checkpoints, and baggage reclaim areas.

Departures (flying out):

  • Enter your terminal and locate your airline's check-in desks using the overhead flight information boards.
  • Check in and drop your hold luggage (most airlines open check-in two to three hours before departure).
  • Proceed through security screening. Remove laptops, liquids (max 100ml per container in a clear bag), and belts. Be prepared for body scanners and random bag checks.
  • Once through security you are in the airside departure lounge. Here you will find restaurants, shops, duty-free, and gate information.
  • Gates are announced approximately 45 to 60 minutes before departure. Note that some Gatwick gates require a bus transfer to remote stands — allow extra walking and transit time.

Arrivals (flying in):

  • After landing, follow signs for Immigration and Border Control. Non-UK/EU passport holders should join the relevant queue. UK and EU citizens can use e-gates where available.
  • Collect your baggage from the designated carousel (check the screens in the baggage reclaim hall for your flight number).
  • Pass through Customs — Green Channel (nothing to declare) or Red Channel (goods to declare).
  • Exit into the Arrivals Hall where your LTR Minicab driver will be waiting with a personalised name board.

Step 7 — Facilities Inside Gatwick to Know Before You Go

Both the North and South Terminals at Gatwick are well-equipped with facilities to make your time airside comfortable and convenient. Knowing what is available helps you make the most of your time before boarding, especially for long-haul travellers with a longer wait.

  • Dining and refreshments: Both terminals offer a wide range of food and drink options airside, from quick-service cafés and fast food to sit-down restaurants. Pre-security, there are also options for travellers dropping off passengers.
  • Duty-free and shopping: Extensive retail including perfumes, spirits, electronics, fashion, and travel accessories. Prices are competitive for international travellers purchasing within allowances.
  • Currency exchange: Multiple currency exchange counters in both terminals, though pre-ordering currency online or using a travel card before you arrive typically gives better rates.
  • Lounges: Business class and premium lounges are available in both terminals, plus pay-per-entry options such as No1 Lounges and Club Aspire for economy travellers seeking a quieter pre-flight experience.
  • Charging stations and Wi-Fi: Free Wi-Fi is available throughout the airport. Charging points for phones and laptops are located in seating areas and departure gates.
  • Family facilities: Dedicated family security lanes, baby changing rooms, nursing areas, children's play zones, and family-friendly seating are all available.
  • Accessibility: Both terminals are fully wheelchair accessible with lifts, assistance desks, accessible toilets, and priority boarding support available on request.
  • Left luggage: Luggage storage is available at both terminals for travellers who need to store bags before or after a flight.
  • Hotels on site: Gatwick has an on-site hotel (Sofitel London Gatwick, connected directly to the South Terminal) and several hotels within a short shuttle distance for overnight stays before early departures.

Gatwick is a hugely popular family holiday departure point, particularly for flights to Spain, Greece, Portugal, and the Caribbean. Travelling as a family or group requires a different level of planning compared to a solo business trip. Here is what to consider:

  • Vehicle size: A standard saloon car carries up to 4 passengers with 2 medium and 2 small bags. For families with 5 or more passengers, pushchairs, or multiple large suitcases, an MPV is a far more practical and comfortable option. LTR Minicab offers MPV 5, MPV 6, MPV 7, Executive MPV 7, and MPV 8 options to suit every group size.
  • Child seats: If travelling with young children, request a child seat or booster seat at the time of booking. LTR Minicab can accommodate this with advance notice.
  • Sports equipment: Travelling with golf clubs, surfboards, skis, or a bicycle? These count as oversized luggage and need to be declared at the time of booking so the correct vehicle size can be assigned.
  • Group coordination: For group travel, a single large MPV is almost always cheaper and more convenient than multiple smaller cabs. LTR Minicab can coordinate multi-vehicle bookings for very large groups travelling together.
  • School holiday periods: During July and August, the Easter fortnight, and the October half-term, Gatwick operates at near-maximum capacity. Book your transfer as early as possible during these periods to guarantee your vehicle type and arrival time.

Step 10 — Tips for a Smooth Gatwick Experience Every Time

Having helped thousands of passengers travel through Gatwick Airport, the LTR Minicab team has compiled a final set of practical tips that make a genuine difference to your airport experience:

  • Always confirm your terminal at the time of booking: North and South terminals have separate drop-off and pickup zones. Confirming the correct terminal at booking prevents any last-minute confusion on the road.
  • Use online check-in: Most airlines flying from Gatwick offer online check-in 24 to 48 hours before departure. Checking in online allows you to go straight to bag drop rather than a full check-in queue, saving up to 30 minutes at the airport.
  • Pack smart for security: Place all liquids (in containers of 100ml or less) in a single clear zip-lock bag before you arrive at the airport. Keep your laptop and any large electronic devices accessible at the top of your hand luggage. This significantly speeds up your security experience.
  • Download your airline app: Most major airlines now offer boarding passes via their app, real-time gate updates, and delay notifications. This is the fastest way to stay informed about your flight status from the moment you leave home.
  • Allow time for remote gates: Gatwick uses several remote stands that require a bus transfer from the terminal to the aircraft. If your gate number indicates a satellite or bus gate, be at your gate at least 20 to 30 minutes before the published boarding time.
  • Keep your driver contact details saved: LTR Minicab provides full driver contact details before your pickup. Save the number in your phone before the day of travel so you can reach your driver instantly if your plans change.
  • For early morning departures: If your flight departs before 6am, consider staying at one of the Gatwick airport hotels the night before, or book your LTR Minicab transfer for a 2am or 3am pickup. Our 24/7 service means we are available for the earliest departures.
  • Travel insurance and documentation: Before you travel anywhere in the world from Gatwick, ensure your travel insurance is active, your passport is valid for at least six months beyond your return date, and any required visas are obtained well in advance. These are the most common causes of preventable travel disruption.
