Discover Hayes UB3 – A Historic West London Town at the Gateway to Heathrow

Hayes UB3 is a large, diverse, and historically significant residential and commercial postcode in the London Borough of Hillingdon, occupying the broad flat corridor between the Grand Union Canal to the east and the Heathrow Airport perimeter to the west. The postcode covers a wide area from the Hayes town centre along Station Road and the High Street northward through the residential streets of Wood End and Yeading to the north, and the quieter Harlington village conservation area and Sipson hamlet to the west, with the Bath Road (A4) commercial corridor forming the southern boundary toward Heston and Cranford. Hayes carries an unusual depth of industrial and cultural heritage for an outer London postcode — the town was home to one of the most important manufacturing and creative centres in twentieth-century Britain, with the EMI factory on Blyth Road producing the recordings of artists including The Beatles, Pink Floyd, Maria Callas, and countless others from the 1930s through to the digital era, and the site of the original Gramophone Company where the iconic His Master's Voice trademark was developed into one of the most recognised brand images in the world.

Hayes & Harlington station on the Elizabeth line provides direct services eastbound into Paddington, Bond Street, Liverpool Street, and Canary Wharf, as well as direct westbound services to Heathrow Terminals 2, 3, 4, and 5 without changing — making UB3 one of the most directly rail-connected postcodes to the airport. The Grand Union Canal towpath provides a continuous cycling and walking corridor from Hayes through Southall and Hanwell into central London, while the A437 and Bath Road give Hayes residents straightforward road access to the Heathrow terminal complex in minutes.
