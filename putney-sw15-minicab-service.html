Discover Putney SW15, South-West London's Thames Riverside Village

SW15 runs along the south bank of the Thames between the Wandsworth and Richmond borders in the London Borough of Wandsworth, a postcode shaped in equal measure by its riverside setting, its green commons, and its strong community character. The area developed as a Thames-side settlement long before it was absorbed into Greater London, and its historic core around Putney High Street and the river retains a distinct identity that separates it from the more commercial south-west London postcodes on its eastern boundary.

Putney Bridge is among the most architecturally distinctive river crossings in London, a Grade II listed stone structure that also carries the District line on a separate lower deck, making it one of only a handful of road bridges in the city with a railway embedded within it. The bridge marks the start of the Oxford and Cambridge University Boat Race, held annually each spring since 1829, with the course running upstream along the tideway to Chiswick Bridge. The race is one of the longest-running sporting fixtures in the world, and on race day the entire length of the Putney Embankment fills with spectators for the start.

The Putney Embankment runs west from the bridge along the riverside, lined with boathouses belonging to the rowing clubs that have defined this stretch of the Thames for well over a century. The concentration of amateur and competitive rowing activity here is among the highest in Britain, and the clubs attract members from across South West London and beyond. Putney Heath begins where the built-up streets of SW15 give way to the south, a 330-acre expanse of ancient heathland that forms the northern portion of the wider Wimbledon and Putney Commons managed under their own Act of Parliament.

Upper Richmond Road provides the commercial spine of the postcode, lined with independent restaurants, cafes, and retailers running from Putney High Street westward through the neighbourhood. The High Street itself connects the Thames frontage to the District line at Putney Bridge station, and the concentration of local businesses, bars, and restaurants in the streets around the junction makes Putney one of the more self-contained and walkable commercial centres in south-west London.
