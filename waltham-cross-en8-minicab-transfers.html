Discover Waltham Cross EN8 – A Historic Lea Valley Town at the Gateway of the M25

Waltham Cross EN8 is a busy and historically layered commuter town in the Broxbourne district of Hertfordshire, occupying the southwestern corner of the county at the point where the River Lea and the Hertfordshire–Essex border converge just north of the M25. The postcode spans from the town centre on the High Street and Eleanor Cross Road northward through the residential streets of Holdbrook, Cheshunt Common, and Flamstead End toward the Goffs Oak boundary, taking in a mixed landscape of inter-war and post-war semis close to the town centre, newer private estates on the northern periphery, and the broad green corridor of the Lee Valley Regional Park along the eastern boundary. Waltham Cross takes its name from one of the twelve Eleanor Crosses erected by King Edward I in 1291–1294 to mark the resting places of the funeral cortège of Queen Eleanor of Castile on its journey from Harby in Nottinghamshire to Westminster Abbey — the Waltham Cross monument, a Grade I listed structure on the High Street, is one of only three surviving Eleanor Crosses in England and represents the most accessible of the three for visitors from the wider north London and Hertfordshire area.

Waltham Cross station and the nearby Theobalds Grove station both provide Lea Valley line services operated by Greater Anglia into Liverpool Street, while the M25 at Junction 25 sits within immediate road reach of the town centre, connecting EN8 residents to the full M25 orbital and giving Waltham Cross one of the most motorway-convenient locations of any Hertfordshire commuter town for long-distance and airport travel.
