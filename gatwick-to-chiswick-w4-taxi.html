The W4 postcode covers a wide and varied stretch of west London sitting between the Thames to the south, Gunnersbury to the west, Acton to the north, and the boundary with W6 Hammersmith to the east, encompassing the whole of Chiswick as one of outer west London's most sought-after and characterful residential districts. The postcode begins at its most prominent point along Chiswick High Road, the neighbourhood's long and commercially varied main street running through the centre of W4 from the Gunnersbury end in the west through to the Chiswick roundabout at the east where it meets the A4. Immediately south of the High Road the streets fan out into a quiet grid including Devonshire Road, Fauconberg Road, Heathfield Terrace, Netheravon Road, Barrowgate Road, Annandale Road, and Edensor Road, all of which form part of the broad Victorian residential area known simply as central Chiswick. Further south the postcode reaches Chiswick Mall, the prestigious riverside road lined with large historic houses and private gardens backing directly onto the Thames at the point where the Oxford Cambridge Boat Race traditionally begins at the Chiswick Bridge end. Running parallel to the river slightly inland is Strand on the Green, a riverside street of charming terraced cottages and converted buildings stretching between Kew Bridge and the railway bridge at Gunnersbury, much favoured for its riverbank pubs and close access to Kew Gardens across the water. Moving north from the High Road the streets become more suburban and include Sutton Court Road, Duke Road, Essex Place, Acton Lane, Hartington Road, Ellesmere Road, Wellesley Road, and Glebe Street as they approach the W4 border with Acton W3. To the west the postcode takes in the Bedford Park area, one of England's earliest planned garden suburbs built in the 1870s and 1880s around the distinctive church of St Michael and All Angels, with Queen Anne and Old English style houses lining Marlborough Crescent, Newton Grove, Priory Gardens, and South Parade. To the east along Burlington Lane and Great Chertsey Road lies Chiswick Business Park, a major commercial campus and the site of Chiswick House, the eighteenth-century Palladian villa set in extensive landscaped grounds that form one of the most visited heritage parks in west London. Hogarth Lane runs alongside the grounds of Hogarth's House, the historic home of the painter William Hogarth. Grove Park occupies the south-western corner of W4 between the High Road and the Thames, a quiet tree-lined residential area centred on Grove Park Road, Hartington Road, and Powell's Walk.
