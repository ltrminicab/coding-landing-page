Discover Blackheath SE3, South-East London's Village on the Heath

Blackheath SE3 occupies a commanding position on the high ground above Greenwich in south-east London, a postcode defined by one of the capital's most recognisable open spaces and a village that has maintained a distinctive character for centuries. The heath itself — a broad expanse of common land stretching across the northern part of the postcode — has been a gathering point since medieval times, famously serving as the muster ground where Wat Tyler's rebels assembled in 1381 and where Henry V was welcomed back by the City of London following the Battle of Agincourt in 1415. Today the heath draws residents for recreation and remains one of the largest areas of accessible open space in south-east London, bordered by Greenwich Park to the west and by Shooters Hill Road to the east.

Blackheath village, centred on Tranquil Vale and Montpelier Row, has developed a reputation as one of south London's most appealing neighbourhood high streets, with a concentration of independent restaurants, specialist retailers, and coffee shops that distinguishes it from more commercial town centres in the surrounding boroughs. The postcode is served by three National Rail stations — Blackheath, Kidbrooke, and Westcombe Park — each providing Southeastern services to London Bridge and Charing Cross, making SE3 well connected by rail for central London commuting. The postcode borders Greenwich SE10 and Lee SE12 and sits within easy reach of Lewisham's DLR interchange to the south-west.
