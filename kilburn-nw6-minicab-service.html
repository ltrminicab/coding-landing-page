Discover Kilburn NW6 – One of London's Most Characterful High Roads

Kilburn NW6 sits astride the A5 — the ancient Watling Street that the Romans drove northwest from London two thousand years ago — and the postcode has been absorbing new communities and reinventing itself ever since. Kilburn High Road runs for close to a mile and a half through the heart of the postcode, packing in independent shops, market stalls, street-food traders, and pubs that range from old-school corner locals to new openings, and giving the area an energy that few stretches of London high street can match. The most visually striking building is the Gaumont State Cinema on Kilburn High Road, an Art Deco tower built in 1937 that seated over 4,000 people and was the largest cinema in Europe when it opened — the auditorium is now a church, but the tower and facade remain a neighbourhood landmark that stops first-time visitors in their tracks. Kiln Theatre, a short walk along the High Road, has built a national reputation for politically engaged and community-rooted theatre since the days when it operated as the Tricycle, drawing audiences from across London and beyond for productions that frequently transfer to the West End. Kilburn has also long been associated with London's Irish community — the nickname "County Kilburn" captures a real pattern of settlement that brought thousands of Irish families to these streets from the 1940s onward, and the pubs and social clubs they established still give parts of NW6 a warmth and familiarity that newer arrivals quickly recognise. Brondesbury Park to the north and the streets around West Hampstead's edge to the east offer leafier residential pockets alongside the bustle of the High Road, making NW6 a postcode that contains more variety than its reputation sometimes suggests.
