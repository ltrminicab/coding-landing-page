Discover Kentish Town NW5, Where Live Music Meets Village Character

Kentish Town NW5 occupies a stretch of North London that has managed to hold onto a neighbourhood feel despite sitting just minutes from Camden Town and a short distance from King's Cross. The high street along Kentish Town Road has been a continuous trading route since at least the medieval period, and the Victorian terraces that line most of the postcode give it a consistent and well-settled visual character that distinguishes it clearly from its louder southern neighbour.

The O2 Forum Kentish Town on Highgate Road is the postcode's most nationally recognised landmark, a 2,300-capacity music venue that has hosted virtually every significant British and international act since it opened in the late nineteenth century. The building itself predates its current use by decades, and its interior has become one of the most familiar live music environments in the capital for anyone who follows touring artists through London.

Kentish Town City Farm on Cressfield Close provides a completely different side of NW5, a free community urban farm with livestock, vegetable plots, and workshops that draws local families and school groups throughout the year. Parliament Hill and the southern edge of Hampstead Heath form the eastern boundary of the postcode, giving residents within easy walking distance one of London's most celebrated open spaces and a hilltop viewpoint that takes in the full London skyline from a single unobstructed vantage point.

Gospel Oak, the northwestern corner of NW5, sits at the terminus of the Gospel Oak to Barking Overground line, one of the few orbital rail routes in London that crosses the capital east-west without passing through any central terminus. The area around Gospel Oak station has a quieter, more residential feel than the Kentish Town Road end of the postcode, with streets of Victorian and Edwardian housing running down toward Dartmouth Park.
