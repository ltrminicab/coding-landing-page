Discover Belgravia SW1W, London's Grand Stucco Quarter

Belgravia SW1W is among the most valuable and visually uniform residential districts in the world, a product of one of the most ambitious planned developments London has ever seen. In the 1820s and 1830s, the builder Thomas Cubitt transformed what had been marshy ground into a grid of wide streets and garden squares faced in white stucco, working under the direction of the Grosvenor estate to create a neighbourhood that would attract the wealthiest residents in the city.

The great squares at the heart of the postcode, including Eaton Square and Chester Square, remain among the most desirable addresses in London and their private central gardens are maintained to an exceptionally high standard. Eaton Square in particular spans a considerable length of the postcode, with the central garden strip running between two rows of stucco-fronted terraces that have changed remarkably little since Cubitt's time.

Elizabeth Street provides the postcode with its main shopping and dining street, a quieter and more independent alternative to the department stores of nearby Knightsbridge. The street is known for its flower stalls, independent bakeries, and specialist food and wine retailers that give Belgravia a village-like character that many residents value precisely because it sits within one of the most central locations in London.

The Grosvenor estate, now managed by the Duke of Westminster, continues to own a substantial proportion of the land and buildings in Belgravia, maintaining a level of physical consistency across the postcode that is essentially unmatched in any other central London district of comparable size. Hyde Park Corner forms the northeastern gateway to SW1W, with Sloane Square marking its southwestern boundary toward Chelsea.
