Discover Cheshunt EN8 – Hertfordshire's Largest Broxbourne Town and the Site of a Tudor Royal Palace

Cheshunt EN8 is the largest settlement in the Broxbourne district of Hertfordshire, occupying an extensive stretch of the A10 corridor between the M25 at Junction 25 to the south and the Hertford–Hoddesdon boundary to the north, covering a broad sweep of residential, commercial, and green-belt landscape that includes the town centre at Turners Hill, the residential districts of Flamstead End, Andrews Lane, Brookfield, Windmill Hill, and Cheshunt Common, and the wooded parkland and reservoir of the former Theobald's estate on the western edge of the postcode. Cheshunt's most celebrated historical association is with Theobalds Palace, the grand Elizabethan mansion built by William Cecil, Lord Burghley — chief minister to Queen Elizabeth I — on the western edge of the town, which subsequently became a favoured residence of King James I after he acquired it from the Cecil family in 1607 and used it as his primary hunting palace throughout much of his reign, a connection that gave Cheshunt a direct place in the court life of early Stuart England. The palace was largely demolished during the Interregnum, though its park landscape survives as Theobald's Park and the adjacent Cedars Park on the southern boundary of Cheshunt adjoining Waltham Cross, preserving the wooded grounds and formal garden setting of one of the most significant royal properties in the Hertfordshire countryside.

Cheshunt station provides frequent Lea Valley line services operated by Greater Anglia into Liverpool Street and northbound into Hertford East, while Waltham Cross and Theobalds Grove stations serve the southern EN8 boundary.
