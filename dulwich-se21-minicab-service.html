Discover Dulwich SE21, South London's Most Distinguished Village

Dulwich SE21 is one of the most characterful and historically significant residential postcodes in inner south London, a postcode defined by its village atmosphere, extensive conservation areas, and a concentration of cultural and educational institutions unmatched in the south of the capital. The postcode covers Dulwich Village, one of the most intact Georgian and Victorian residential streetscapes in London, and extends across the grounds of Dulwich Park and into the wooded ridge of Sydenham Hill, where ancient woodland and large Victorian properties mark the southern boundary of SE21. The entire area developed under the Dulwich Estate, a charitable trust established by the actor-manager Edward Alleyn in 1619 when he founded Dulwich College on land he had acquired in the parish, and the Estate continues to exercise planning restrictions that have preserved the character of the village and its surroundings to a remarkable degree.

Dulwich Picture Gallery, opened in 1817 to designs by Sir John Soane, is the oldest public art gallery in England and houses one of the finest collections of Old Master paintings in the country, including works by Rembrandt, Rubens, Gainsborough, and Poussin. The Gallery building itself is considered one of the masterpieces of English neoclassical architecture, and its design directly influenced the layout of the National Gallery on Trafalgar Square. Dulwich Park, a 72-acre Victorian public park adjacent to the Gallery and the College, provides one of the most formally landscaped open spaces in inner south London, with a lake, boathouse, café, and ornamental gardens maintained by the London Borough of Southwark.
