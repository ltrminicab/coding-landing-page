Step 1 — Which Airport Should You Fly Into?

London is served by four major international airports: Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, and Luton. The right one for you depends on where you are flying from and which part of London you plan to explore. Here is a clear, honest breakdown of each.

LHR · West London

London Heathrow

Best for: Long-haul travellers from USA, Middle East, Asia, Africa, South America, and Australasia. Served by British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, Emirates, Qatar Airways, American Airlines, Etihad, Cathay Pacific, Air India, PIA, and 80+ more. Located 14 miles west of central London.

LGW · South London

London Gatwick

Best for: European travellers, holiday packages, and some long-haul leisure routes (Caribbean, USA, Far East). Served by easyJet, TUI, Norwegian, Turkish Airlines, Pegasus, Emirates, and charter carriers. Located 26 miles south of central London.

STN · North-East London

London Stansted

Best for: Budget travellers from Europe — especially Eastern Europe, Southern Europe, and Scandinavia. Ryanair's main hub, plus Wizz Air, Jet2, and other low-cost carriers. Located 38 miles north-east of central London.

LTN · North London

London Luton

Best for: Budget flights across Europe and some Middle East routes. Served by easyJet, Wizz Air, Ryanair, TUI, and Jet2. Located 32 miles north of central London.

Step 3 — The Best Places to Visit in London This Summer

With your airport chosen and hotel booked, here is your curated guide to the very best London summer experiences — from iconic royal landmarks to hidden green spaces, world-class museums, and vibrant neighbourhood markets.

  • 1
    Buckingham Palace & St James's Park

    Watch the Changing of the Guard at 11am and stroll through one of London's most beautiful royal parks. In July and August, the State Rooms open to the public — a rare chance to explore inside. Free (park) · Paid (State Rooms).

  • 2
    Tower of London & Tower Bridge

    London's most visited paid attraction, welcoming 2.9 million visitors in 2024. Home to the Crown Jewels, nearly 1,000 years of history, and the famous Tower Ravens. Cross the bridge on foot for spectacular Thames views. Book tickets in advance.

  • 3
    The London Eye & South Bank

    A 30-minute capsule ride gives you a 360° view across the city. The South Bank promenade stretching from Westminster to London Bridge is at its absolute best in summer — street food, performers, and terrace cafés all along the riverside. Book online to save.

  • 4
    Hyde Park & BST Festival

    London's most iconic royal park hosts the BST Hyde Park concert series each summer — a 2–3 week festival of world-class live music in a stunning outdoor setting. Beyond the concerts, the Serpentine Lido, boat hire, and open lawns are perfect for a summer afternoon. Free entry (concerts ticketed).

  • 5
    The British Museum

    One of the world's greatest museums and entirely free to enter. The Rosetta Stone, Egyptian mummies, the Elgin Marbles, and the Sutton Hoo helmet are just a few of the eight million objects on display. Book a timed entry slot in advance during summer.

  • 6
    Greenwich & the Royal Observatory

    Stand on the Prime Meridian line (0° longitude), visit the Cutty Sark clipper ship, and enjoy one of the most stunning panoramic views of the London skyline from the top of Greenwich Park. A UNESCO World Heritage Site that deserves a full half-day. Best reached by taxi or river boat.

Step 4 — What to Visit Based on Your Arrival Airport

Your arrival airport is not just a gateway — it positions you closer to certain parts of London. Here is how to make the most of each landing point, and which attractions are most accessible from each.

Flying into Heathrow (LHR) — West London Gateway

Heathrow is ideally positioned for West and Central London. Arriving here, you are closest to Kensington, Chelsea, Notting Hill, Hyde Park, and the West End. With LTR Minicab based just 0.5 miles from Terminal 5, your transfer into London begins the moment you clear customs.

  • First stop from Heathrow: Kensington Palace and Hyde Park (25–30 min by taxi)
  • Day trip out: Windsor Castle and Hampton Court Palace (both 20–30 min)
  • Best hotel area: Kensington (W8), Notting Hill (W11), or Paddington (W2)
  • Transfer advantage: Fixed price guaranteed from Terminal 2, 3, 4 or 5 — no metered surprises after a long flight
Recommend Heathrow for: USA · Canada · UAE · Pakistan · India · Australia · Japan · South Korea · Nigeria · South Africa · Bangladesh · China · Hong Kong · Brazil · Singapore

Flying into Gatwick (LGW) — South London Gateway

Gatwick is 26 miles south of central London and well-placed for South London attractions, Crystal Palace, Brixton, and a direct rail connection to Victoria station (Gatwick Express, ~30 minutes). From Victoria, the whole of London opens up easily.

  • First stop from Gatwick: Victoria station area, then Westminster or South Bank
  • Day trip out: Brighton and the Sussex coast (30 min from Gatwick by train)
  • Best hotel area: Victoria (SW1), Battersea, or Covent Garden (after a short transfer)
  • Transfer: Fixed transfer from Gatwick to any London hotel or attraction — from £65
Recommend Gatwick for: Italy · Spain · Portugal · Turkey · France · Caribbean · USA (budget) · Greece · Morocco · Egypt · Netherlands · Germany (leisure)

Flying into Stansted (STN) — North-East Gateway

Stansted is the furthest London airport from the centre at 38 miles, but it is Ryanair's main UK hub and handles a huge volume of European budget flights. From Stansted, the Stansted Express train reaches Liverpool Street in central London in about 47 minutes. Alternatively, a direct minicab takes you wherever you need to go — without any luggage hassle on busy trains.

  • First stop from Stansted: Shoreditch and the City of London (accessible from Liverpool Street)
  • Day trip out: Cambridge is only 30 minutes from Stansted by train
  • Best hotel area: Shoreditch (E1/EC2), Liverpool Street, or Bloomsbury
  • Transfer: Fixed transfer from Stansted — from £75 to central London
Recommend Stansted for: Poland · Czech Republic · Hungary · Romania · Lithuania · Latvia · Estonia · Scandinavia (budget) · Slovakia · Serbia · Ukraine · Bulgaria · Croatia

Flying into Luton (LTN) — North Gateway

Luton is primarily a budget airline hub, sitting 32 miles north of London. A shuttle connects Luton Airport Parkway to St Pancras International in around 22 minutes via Thameslink. From St Pancras, the British Museum, Camden, and King's Cross are all within easy reach. A minicab gives you the added comfort of door-to-door service — especially welcome with luggage after a budget flight.

  • First stop from Luton: King's Cross / St Pancras area, then British Museum or Camden Market
  • Day trip out: Oxford is easily accessible from London, or visit Blenheim Palace
  • Best hotel area: King's Cross (N1), Bloomsbury (WC1), or Islington
  • Transfer: Fixed transfer from Luton — from £68 to central London
Recommend Luton for: Morocco · Algeria · Tunisia · Romania · Bulgaria · Lebanon · Jordan · Poland (budget) · Malta · Cyprus · Iceland · Ireland · Portugal (budget)
