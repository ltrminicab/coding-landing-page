The E20 postcode covers the whole of the Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park and the new urban neighbourhoods that have grown up around it in Stratford, forming one of the largest and most visited regeneration developments in London's modern history. The postcode came into existence specifically to serve this new district and sits within the London Borough of Newham, bounded roughly by the River Lea and its tributaries to the west and south, the Stratford High Street corridor to the south-east, and the Hackney Wick and Pudding Mill Lane areas to the north. The heart of the postcode is the park itself, a 560-acre managed greenspace and events venue built on land formerly occupied by industrial works and waterlogged marshland in the lower Lea Valley. Within the park the major permanent structures include the London Stadium on the western edge of the park, originally built as the 2012 Olympic athletics venue and now the home ground of West Ham United Football Club and a major concert and events arena with a capacity of 60,000 and above for the largest events. The Aquatics Centre, designed by Zaha Hadid and completed for the 2012 Games, sits immediately south of the Velodrome, which operates as the Lee Valley VeloPark and is open for public cycling and competitive events. The Copper Box Arena is used for a wide range of community and professional sports including boxing, handball, and fitness. The ArcelorMittal Orbit sculpture and observation tower stands prominently at the southern end of the park and includes one of the world's longest tunnel slides descending from the viewing platform. Running along the eastern perimeter of the park is the Here East campus, a converted broadcast centre from the 2012 Games that now serves as a technology, innovation, and media hub housing universities, startups, and established tech businesses. The residential side of E20 is concentrated across several distinct neighbourhoods. East Village, the largest of these, was originally the Athletes' Village during the 2012 Games and has since been converted into a substantial residential community of over 2,800 homes along the streets of Celebration Avenue, Honour Lea Avenue, Cheering Lane, Montfichet Road, and Liberty Bridge Road, with shops, cafes, and a central park creating a self-contained urban neighbourhood immediately east of the Olympic Stadium. Chobham Manor to the north-east is a newer residential development of family homes and apartments being built out in phases along the north side of the park, with Sweetwater a further development of townhouses and flats currently under construction or recently completed in the northern section of the park. The southern approach to the park along Stratford High Street and Great Eastern Road connects the E20 boundary to Stratford town centre, while Westfield Stratford City, one of Europe's largest urban shopping centres, sits on the western edge of the postcode boundary at the junction with E15. Stratford station lies just outside the E20 boundary at the junction with E15 and E7, but is the primary rail gateway for the park providing Elizabeth line, Jubilee line, DLR, and London Overground services, while Stratford International station within E20 itself provides High Speed 1 domestic connections to St Pancras.
