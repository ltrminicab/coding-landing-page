Discover Croydon CR0, South London's Thriving Commercial Hub

Croydon CR0 is one of the largest and most commercially significant postcodes in Greater London, covering an expansive area that stretches from the dense urban centre of Croydon town to the residential neighbourhoods of Thornton Heath, Norbury, Addiscombe, and Shirley. Croydon itself is the largest town in Surrey by population and one of the most important commercial centres in south London, with a skyline of office towers that makes it unique among outer London districts and a retail offer centred on the Whitgift and Centrale shopping complexes that draw shoppers from across the southern suburbs. The town centre is currently undergoing one of the most ambitious regeneration programmes in the UK, with the Westfield and Hammerson Croydon retail development and a series of large-scale residential and commercial schemes reshaping the area around East Croydon station and along the Wellesley Road corridor.

East Croydon station is one of the busiest railway stations outside central London, serving Thameslink services to Luton and St Pancras via London Bridge and the City, and Southern services to London Victoria, Brighton, and Gatwick Airport. The Tramlink network — London's only tram system — connects Croydon to Wimbledon in the west, Beckenham in the east, and New Addington and Elmers End in the southeast, providing a distinctive mode of local transport that supplements the extensive bus and rail network. Croydon also has deep historical roots as an archiepiscopal seat — the Old Palace of the Archbishops of Canterbury in the town centre dates from the 15th century — and as the site of the world's first commercial airport, Croydon Airport, which operated between 1920 and 1959 and is now commemorated at the Croydon Airport Visitor Centre in Wallington.

  • Croydon Minster – the medieval parish church of St John the Baptist on Church Street, one of the oldest buildings in Croydon, substantially rebuilt in the 19th century after a fire and containing tombs of several Archbishops of Canterbury who resided at the adjacent Old Palace
  • Surrey Street Market – one of London's oldest and most continuously operating street markets, trading on Surrey Street since at least the 13th century and continuing to provide fresh produce, food, and goods six days a week in the heart of the town centre
