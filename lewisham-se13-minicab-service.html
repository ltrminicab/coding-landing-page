Discover Lewisham SE13, South-East London's Ravensbourne Valley Hub

SE13 occupies the centre of the London Borough of Lewisham on the south-east side of the capital, a postcode defined by its position in the valley of the River Ravensbourne and its role as the principal transport interchange for a wide section of inner south-east London. The area has been a settlement since at least the medieval period, when the village of Lewisham stood at a crossing of the Ravensbourne on the road south from London into Kent, and the town centre that developed from that original village now forms one of the most active and self-contained local high streets in the inner south-east of the city.

Lewisham station opened in 1849 on the South Eastern Railway route connecting London to Folkestone and the Kent coast. The station grew significantly through the Victorian and Edwardian periods as the surrounding residential streets were built out, and by the late 20th century it had become one of the busiest interchange points in south-east London. The arrival of the DLR extension to Lewisham in 1999 added a direct high-frequency connection to the Docklands, Canary Wharf, and the City of London that transformed the station into a major multi-modal hub and drew significant commercial and residential investment to the town centre throughout the 2000s and 2010s.

Ladywell Fields runs along the western boundary of SE13, a linear park following the course of the River Ravensbourne from Catford northward through Ladywell toward Lewisham town centre. The park was substantially improved in the 2000s and now provides one of the most accessible stretches of riverside open space in inner south-east London, with cycling and walking paths, landscaped planting, and restored riverbank running the full length of the corridor. The Ravensbourne itself, once heavily culverted and channelled through the industrial parts of the town, has been partially restored in sections of the park to a more natural meandering course.

Hither Green occupies the southern part of SE13, a quieter residential area centred on Hither Green station and the streets running south from the town centre. The area contains some of the best-preserved Edwardian and early Victorian residential streets in the borough, and the Manor House Gardens at the southern edge of SE13 provide a formal Victorian park with a lake, café, and open lawns that serve as a local counterpart to the riverside parks to the north.
