Discover Bow E3 – East London's Historic Riverside Community Beside Victoria Park and the Lea Valley

Bow E3 is one of inner east London's most historically significant and geographically distinctive postcodes — a neighbourhood shaped by the River Lea, the industrial heritage of the Lea Valley, the vast green expanse of Victoria Park, and a community tradition stretching back through centuries of East End working life that makes it one of the most authentic and characterful areas in the entire capital. The postcode sits in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets, immediately west of Stratford and north of Poplar, forming a broadly triangular territory bounded by Victoria Park to the north, the River Lea to the east, and Mile End Road to the south — a geography that gives E3 its distinctive character as a neighbourhood simultaneously connected to the dynamism of the Olympic Park and the deep-rooted community life of the traditional East End. Victoria Park itself — often referred to as the People's Park — is one of London's oldest and most beloved open spaces, opened to the public in 1845 in direct response to petitions from overcrowded East End residents who desperately needed recreational land, and stretching across 86 hectares of landscaped grounds, lakes, ornamental features, and sports facilities that continue to serve millions of local visitors every year. The park has hosted some of east London's most significant community gatherings, political meetings, and large-scale music events throughout its history, and the annual All Points East music festival in the park regularly attracts headline international acts and tens of thousands of visitors to Bow each summer.

Among the most extraordinary and historically resonant sites in the entire E3 postcode is Three Mills Island, a small island formed in the River Lea at Bromley-by-Bow that is home to the House Mill — the largest surviving tidal mill in the world, built in 1776, which once formed part of one of the most important industrial milling complexes in England and ground grain for London's gin distillers and bakers for over a century before its eventual conversion into a heritage site managed by the River Lea Tidal Mill Trust. Alongside the House Mill stands the Clock Mill, also Grade II* listed, and Three Mills Studios — one of London's leading film and television production facilities, occupying the historic industrial buildings and wharf structures of the island and hosting productions ranging from major feature films to network television series. The River Lea flowing through and around E3 is also part of the Lee Valley Regional Park, a vast green corridor running northward through the capital that offers extensive cycling, walking, and water sports alongside its rich industrial archaeology. Bow's social history is equally significant: the 1888 Matchgirls' Strike, led by workers at the Bryant and May factory on Fairfield Road in Bow, was one of the most important and successful examples of early trade union organising in British history, and is commemorated as a pivotal moment in the development of modern workers' rights. This layering of industrial heritage, community activism, outstanding parkland, and contemporary creative industry gives Bow E3 a depth of character that sets it apart from most other east London postcodes of comparable size.

District line and DLR services from Bow connect passengers into central London, from where the Heathrow Express connecting Paddington to Heathrow provides the fastest rail link — but E3 passengers still face a lengthy multi-stage journey across busy central London stations while managing airport luggage well before they reach any direct Heathrow rail service.

Black Cab

Black taxis operating in east London are available on Bow Road and surrounding main streets, but they operate on a running meter that accumulates continuously throughout the journey. A 14 to 15-mile metered cross-London trip via the A40 to Heathrow during peak hours can produce a final fare well above the fixed price of a pre-booked private minicab, with no certainty about the amount until the journey has ended.

No advance booking required Metered fare uncertainty
