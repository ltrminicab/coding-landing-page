Discover London Bridge SE1, the South Bank's Historic and Cultural Heart

SE1 is among the most visited and most historically layered postcodes in Britain, stretching along the south bank of the Thames from the approaches to Tower Bridge in the east to the South Bank arts complex at Waterloo in the west. The postcode sits in the London Borough of Southwark, one of the oldest administrative districts in the city, and its boundaries encompass a concentration of cultural landmarks, food markets, railway termini, and river crossings that give it a character entirely distinct from any other inner London postcode.

London Bridge itself is the oldest river crossing in the capital, with a bridge documented at or near this location since Roman times, though the current structure dates to 1973. The bridge connects the City of London on the north bank to Borough and Southwark on the south, and the station beneath it is one of the busiest railway terminals in Europe, handling millions of commuters and visitors passing through SE1 every year. Adjacent to the station, The Shard rises 310 metres above the south bank to become Western Europe's tallest building, housing offices, a hotel, restaurants, and observation decks that offer unobstructed views across the city in every direction.

Borough Market occupies the arches and streets immediately south of London Bridge station, a food market with documented origins on this site since at least the 11th century and one of the oldest continuously operating markets in Britain. The current market draws specialist food producers, street food traders, and visitors throughout the week, and the surrounding streets of Borough have developed into one of the most concentrated restaurant and bar districts in inner south London. Bermondsey Street to the southeast of the station is one of the most distinctive independent-business streets in London, lined with galleries, restaurants, and shops in a Victorian railway arch setting.

Tate Modern occupies the former Bankside Power Station on the western stretch of SE1's riverside, its iconic chimney and turbine hall forming the largest modern art museum in the world by visitor numbers. Shakespeare's Globe sits immediately to its east, a faithful reconstruction of the 1599 Globe Theatre completed in 1997 and offering outdoor performances of Shakespeare's works throughout the summer on the bank of the Thames. The Southbank Centre at the western end of the postcode contains the Royal Festival Hall, Queen Elizabeth Hall, and the Hayward Gallery, alongside the National Theatre and BFI Southbank immediately east of Waterloo Bridge.
