Discover Hoddesdon EN11 – A Hertfordshire Market Town Steeped in History on the River Lea

Hoddesdon EN11 is a historic market town in the Broxbourne district of Hertfordshire, straddling the A10 trunk road along the western bank of the River Lea approximately 17 miles north of London and 4 miles north of the M25 at Junction 25. The postcode covers the compact town centre on the High Street and Burford Street northward through the residential streets of Rye Park and Dobbs Weir, taking in the broad meander of the River Lea and the Rye House Meadows nature reserve on the eastern boundary, with established inter-war and post-war housing stock giving way to more recent development on the northern and western edges of the town. Hoddesdon's most celebrated historical landmark is Rye House, a 15th-century brick gatehouse and fortified manor on the River Lea at Rye Park whose surviving masonry is all that remains of the larger castle that was the scene of the Rye House Plot of 1683 — a conspiracy to assassinate King Charles II and James, Duke of York, as the royal party returned from Newmarket — an episode that brought Hoddesdon into the political history of the later Stuart period and whose Grade I listed gatehouse remains one of the most significant surviving medieval structures in Hertfordshire.

Rye House station in the south of the town provides frequent Lea Valley line services operated by Greater Anglia southbound to Broxbourne, Tottenham Hale, and Liverpool Street, while Broxbourne station is a short distance away for additional onward connections. For Heathrow travel the M25 at Junction 25 is the key access point, reached via the A10 southbound through Waltham Cross in approximately 10 to 15 minutes from the EN11 town centre. Hoddesdon EN11 – Area Highlights

  • Rye House, the 15th-century fortified brick gatehouse on the River Lea at Rye Park which is the surviving remnant of the manor associated with the Rye House Plot of 1683 — the conspiracy to assassinate King Charles II — and is today a Grade I listed ancient monument managed by English Heritage as one of the most historically significant medieval structures in the Lea Valley and the wider Hertfordshire countryside
  • Rye House Meadows Site of Special Scientific Interest, the riverside wetland and floodplain meadow habitat along the eastern edge of Hoddesdon EN11 managed as a nature reserve within the Lee Valley Regional Park, supporting nationally important populations of breeding wading birds and wetland plants in a landscape preserved from development along the Hertfordshire–Essex boundary
  • Lee Valley Regional Park, the 26-mile linear park running along the River Lea through the full length of the eastern EN11 boundary, providing extensive walking, cycling, water sports, and nature reserve access from Hoddesdon southward through Waltham Cross, Waltham Abbey, and into the north London reaches of the Lea Valley corridor
  • Dobbs Weir, the well-known stretch of the River Lea at the southern boundary of Hoddesdon with a historic lock, weir, and riverside public house that forms a popular leisure destination for Broxbourne district residents and provides one of the most picturesque riverine settings in the Hertfordshire Lea Valley accessible directly from the EN11 postcode
  • Lowewood Museum, the local history museum housed within a Georgian townhouse on the Hoddesdon High Street, preserving the civic and social history of Hoddesdon and the Broxbourne district through its collections of maps, artefacts, photographs, and records documenting the town's development from its medieval market origins through to its role as a modern Hertfordshire commuter settlement
  • John Eliot Almshouses, the early 17th-century historic almshouses in the Hoddesdon town centre, a listed building forming part of the architectural heritage of the High Street alongside the former Clock House and the Victorian and Edwardian commercial frontages that give Hoddesdon its distinctive market-town character among the Broxbourne district settlements of the Lea Valley
