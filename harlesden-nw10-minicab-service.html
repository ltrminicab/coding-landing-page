Discover Harlesden NW10, the Reggae Capital of Europe

Harlesden NW10 has a character unlike anywhere else in northwest London, shaped above all by the Caribbean communities who began settling here during the Windrush era of the 1950s and built a neighbourhood that became the beating heart of British reggae and sound system culture by the 1970s.

The postcode earned the nickname the reggae capital of Europe through record shops, studios, and venues along Harlesden High Street and the surrounding streets that produced and promoted Jamaican music at a time when it had almost no mainstream platform in Britain. That heritage is still present today in the businesses, community organisations, and cultural memory that makes NW10 one of the most distinctly characterful postcodes in London.

Roundwood Park, a Victorian municipal park off Harlesden High Street, provides the postcode with over 28 acres of formal gardens and open grassland that host community events and seasonal fairs throughout the year. The park's original layout from the 1890s is largely intact, making it one of the better-preserved Victorian parks in the borough of Brent.

Immediately south of Harlesden, the Old Oak Common area is undergoing the largest regeneration project in London outside of the Olympic Park, with 26,000 new homes planned around the new Elizabeth line station that opened at Old Oak Common in 2022. The transformation of this formerly industrial corridor is already reshaping the southern edge of NW10 and connecting Harlesden to the wider city in ways that will continue to develop over the coming decade.
