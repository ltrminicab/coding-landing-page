Discover Muswell Hill N10, North London's Hilltop Village

N10 occupies one of the highest ridgelines in Greater London, running along the crest of the hills that separate the valleys of the Lee to the east and the Brent to the west, in the London Borough of Haringey. The postcode was developed largely in the Edwardian period as a planned suburb on rural land north of Hornsey, giving it a character unlike most inner North London postcodes. The result is one of the most architecturally cohesive shopping streets in the capital, with the terracotta-clad Muswell Hill Broadway forming the commercial heart of the area in a style that is instantly recognisable and largely unchanged since it was built around 1900.

Alexandra Palace stands at the southern edge of N10, occupying 196 acres of parkland that slopes down from the ridge toward Alexandra Park below. The original building was opened in 1873 and destroyed by fire just 16 days later, with the rebuilt palace opening in 1875 and operating continuously since as a venue for exhibitions, concerts, and public events. It was from Alexandra Palace that the BBC made the world's first regular high-definition television broadcast in November 1936, one of the most significant technical firsts in the history of broadcasting. The Great Hall today holds over 10,000 people for major concerts and touring shows, while the ice rink, boating lake, and panoramic terrace draw visitors throughout the year.

Highgate Wood lies at the southern boundary of N10, a 70-acre ancient woodland managed by the City of London Corporation that has been documented as woodland since the medieval period. The wood contains oak, hornbeam, and holly, and the popular cafe at its centre draws local families throughout the year. Adjacent to it and connected by pedestrian paths is Queens Wood, a 52-acre ancient woodland that falls partly within N10 and partly within the neighbouring N6 postcode, giving Muswell Hill a green southern boundary that few other North London postcodes can match.

Fortis Green Road runs east from the Broadway through the centre of N10, lined with independent businesses, cafes, and residential streets that define much of the area's neighbourhood character. The streets north of the Broadway, including Grand Avenue, Dukes Avenue, and Pages Lane, contain some of the best-preserved Edwardian housing stock in North London, and the combination of tree-lined roads and period architecture makes N10 one of the more sought-after residential addresses in the borough.
