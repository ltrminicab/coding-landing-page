The E1W postcode covers Wapping in full, a narrow strip of riverside London running between St Katharine Docks and Tower Bridge in the west and Limehouse and Shadwell in the east, bordered by the Thames to the south and Cable Street and the A1203 to the north. At its heart runs Wapping High Street, a long, atmospheric road lined with converted Victorian warehouses, many now luxury apartments, that once formed the centre of London's docklands trade. Running parallel and connecting to it are Wapping Wall, Garnet Street, Reardon Street, Cinnamon Street, and Tench Street, all part of the dense network of former dock buildings and worker's cottages that have been transformed into residential blocks while retaining much of their original character. Wapping Lane forms the main north to south spine through the postcode, linking The Highway in the north down to the river, passing close to Wapping station on the London Overground. Along The Highway sit Pennington Street and the entrance to Tobacco Dock, a Grade I listed former warehouse complex now used as an events and exhibition venue, surrounded by newer apartment developments. To the east, the postcode reaches towards King Edward Memorial Park, a riverside green space popular for walks along the Thames Path, and on toward Shadwell Basin, a former dock now used for water sports and ringed by modern housing. Thomas More Street and the area around St Katharine Docks mark the western edge of E1W, where converted warehouses sit alongside the marina and give residents easy walking access to Tower Bridge and the City of London. Throughout E1W, narrow cobbled side streets, hidden courtyards, and converted dock buildings sit close to busy main roads, and LTR Minicab drivers know how to navigate every one of them to bring you directly to your entrance.
