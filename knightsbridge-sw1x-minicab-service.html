Discover Knightsbridge SW1X, London's Global Shopping Capital

Knightsbridge SW1X holds a position in London that is almost unique among the world's great cities, a postcode whose identity is built almost entirely around retail, hospitality, and the global clientele that both attract. The area takes its name from a medieval bridge over the Westbourne river, a crossing that once marked the western edge of the city, but its modern character dates from the Victorian era when the construction of Harrods and the gradual arrival of luxury hotels transformed it into one of the most visited addresses on earth.

Harrods on Brompton Road is the defining landmark of the postcode. Founded by Charles Henry Harrod in 1849 as a small grocery, it grew across the second half of the nineteenth century into a department store covering 4.5 acres across seven floors, with over 300 individual departments including its famous food halls, the Egyptian escalators installed in 1914, and a retail operation that today draws visitors from every country in the world. The store's terracotta facade, illuminated by thousands of lights each evening, is among the most photographed commercial buildings in London.

Harvey Nichols at the eastern end of Brompton Road provides a different but equally established luxury retail proposition, having traded from its present corner position since 1831 and now operating as one of the leading fashion and lifestyle department stores in Europe. Between the two anchor stores, Sloane Street stretches south from Knightsbridge station past the showrooms of virtually every major European fashion house, making the street one of the most concentrated strips of luxury retail anywhere in the world.

Hyde Park forms the northern boundary of SW1X, with the cavalry barracks on South Carriage Drive sitting at the point where the park meets the postcode. The park's southeastern corner, including the Serpentine and the Diana Memorial Fountain, is within easy walking distance of most Knightsbridge addresses and gives the area an immediate access to open space that very few central London postcodes of comparable commercial intensity can match.
