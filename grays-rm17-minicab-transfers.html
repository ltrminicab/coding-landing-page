Discover Grays RM17 – Thurrock's Thames-Side Principal Town at the Gateway of the M25

Grays RM17 is the principal town and administrative centre of the Thurrock unitary authority in Essex, occupying a prominent position on the north bank of the River Thames approximately 20 miles east of central London and directly north of the Dartford Crossing, with the M25 at Junction 30 sitting just two miles west of the town centre along the A1306 corridor. The postcode covers the compact town centre around Grays High Street and the civic quarter, the residential districts of Little Thurrock, Socketts Heath, and Badgers Dene to the north and east, and the industrial and logistics corridor along the A1306 and River Thames waterfront toward West Thurrock and the Lakeside retail complex at Junction 30 of the M25. Grays has been a significant Thames-side settlement since the medieval period, its name derived from the de Grai family who held the manor in the 13th century, and the town's position at the intersection of the Thames waterway and the principal Essex–London road routes gave it an important role in the movement of goods and passengers between the Essex hinterland and the capital throughout the pre-railway era, a function now continued by the C2C rail corridor and the M25/A13 motorway network.

Grays station is served by C2C rail services operated by Trenitalia, running westbound into Fenchurch Street and eastbound into Southend — the Fenchurch Street line provides connections to the City and DLR interchange at Limehouse, though neither route offers a practical Heathrow connection. For Heathrow travel the M25 at Junction 30 is the defining access point for RM17, reached within minutes of leaving the town centre via the A1306, and placing Grays among the most motorway-advantaged towns in the eastern home counties for westbound airport transfers.
