The HA1 postcode is built around two distinct halves of Harrow, the historic hilltop town of Harrow on the Hill and the busier modern centre of Harrow town itself. Harrow on the Hill sits at the top of the postcode, home to the famous Harrow School founded in 1572, narrow cobbled streets, the parish church of St Mary's with its commanding views across London, and a tight cluster of period houses and independent shops along the High Street. Down the hill towards the east, Greenhill and Harrow town centre form the main shopping and transport hub, centred on St Ann's shopping centre, St George's shopping centre, and the pedestrianised College Road and Station Road. South of the town centre, Roxeth and Sudbury Hill stretch towards South Harrow, a largely residential area of interwar semis along Sudbury Hill, Bessborough Road, and Northolt Road. To the west, West Harrow sits between the railway lines, a quiet residential pocket close to West Harrow station, while the northern edge of HA1 blends into Harrow Weald and Wealdstone, with their own high streets and stations a short distance away. The A404 Pinner Road and the A4005 Sheepcote Road provide the main routes through HA1 towards the A40 and onward connections across west London.
